# New Typography

## summary
The New Typography was a graphic design movement based in the Weimar Republic, defined in 1928 by the typographer Jan Tschichold in his book Die Neue Typographie. It rejected the traditional arrangement of type in favour of asymmetry, sans-serif letterforms and the active combination of photography and text, applied to posters, advertisements, business cards, stationery and brochures. Its participants included El Lissitzky, Kurt Schwitters, László Moholy-Nagy and Aleksandr Rodchenko, who held that graphic design could change society and convey the dynamism of modern life.
