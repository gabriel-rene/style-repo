# Cyanotype

## summary
The cyanotype is a photographic process devised by Sir John Herschel in 1841 that produces a continuous-tone image in Prussian blue. It relies on a sensitising solution of ferric ammonium citrate and potassium ferricyanide, whose iron salts are reduced when exposed to natural or artificial ultraviolet light and then yield a high-contrast blue image on oxidation. The process was long used to copy engineering and architectural drawings before photocopiers became common, and photographers also used it for botanical records, still lifes and proof prints from negatives.
