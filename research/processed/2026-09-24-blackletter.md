# Blackletter

## summary
Blackletter, often popularly called Gothic, began as a medieval book hand of the Gothic script family, known as Textualis or Textura, and was later turned into typefaces that remain in use in calligraphy and typesetting. The script arose in the late twelfth century and was used across Western Europe until around the seventeenth century. Johannes Gutenberg cut a textualis type, rich in ligatures and abbreviations, for his 42-line Bible. The Fraktur variant became the most common German blackletter by the middle of the sixteenth century.
