# Ligne claire

## summary
Ligne claire, or the Clear Line, names the clarity of drawing, composition and narrative that Hergé developed while creating the Tintin adventures. After World War II a group of contributors to Tintin Weekly followed his principles and became known as the Hergé School, later the Brussels School, including Edgar P. Jacobs and Bob de Moor. The Dutch label Klare Lijn appeared in print in February 1977, when Joost Swarte used it as the title of a booklet for a Rotterdam Tintin exhibition. The approach was then reinterpreted by Dutch underground and French-language comics artists.
