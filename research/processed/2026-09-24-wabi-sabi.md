# Wabi-sabi

## summary
Wabi-sabi is a concept in traditional Japanese aesthetics centered on accepting transience and imperfection, valuing beauty that is imperfect, impermanent and incomplete. It stems from the Buddhist teaching of the three marks of existence and was shaped in the tea ceremony by Murata Jukō, who favored simple rough utensils, and by Sen no Rikyū through his teahouse design. Its qualities include asymmetry, roughness, simplicity, economy, austerity and an appreciation of natural forms. Designer Leonard Koren's 1994 book contrasted it with Western ideals of beauty and became a reference point for designers.
