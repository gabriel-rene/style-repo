# Skeuomorphism

## summary
Skeuomorphism is a design practice that brings real-world elements into digital interfaces so that new interfaces feel familiar; the term can mean either a functional learning aid or a visual trend of unnecessary real-world detail. The trend grew in the early 2000s and peaked in the early 2010s, with interfaces using realistic textures, shadows and gradients for a three-dimensional effect, such as wooden bookshelves in an e-reader or leather-bound notepads. Its excesses came to seem cluttered and dated, and flat design emerged in response.
